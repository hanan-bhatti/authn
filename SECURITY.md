# Security Policy

The Authn team takes security seriously. We appreciate your efforts to responsibly disclose your findings, and we will make every effort to acknowledge your contributions.

## 🛡️ Security Philosophy

- **Defense in Depth:** We employ multiple layers of security controls, from network-level protections to application-level logic.
- **Principle of Least Privilege:** Services and users are granted only the permissions necessary to perform their functions.
- **Secure Defaults:** The default configuration is designed to be secure out-of-the-box.
- **Transparency:** We believe in being transparent about our security practices and any vulnerabilities that are discovered and patched.

## 🕵️ Reporting a Vulnerability

We take all security reports seriously. If you believe you have found a security vulnerability in Authn, please **do not** disclose it publicly by opening a GitHub issue or posting on public forums.

Instead, we ask you to send a private email to:

**hannanbhatti2006@gmail.com**

Please provide a detailed report with the following information:
- A clear and concise description of the vulnerability.
- Step-by-step instructions to reproduce the issue.
- The potential impact of the vulnerability (e.g., data exposure, privilege escalation).
- Any proof-of-concept, scripts, or screenshots that would help us understand and fix the issue.

## timeline

Upon receiving a security report, our team will strive to follow this timeline:

1.  **Acknowledgement (1-2 business days):** We will confirm that we have received your report.
2.  **Initial Triage (3-5 business days):** Our team will investigate the report to validate the vulnerability. We will communicate our findings and the expected severity level.
3.  **Remediation (As soon as possible):** We will develop and test a patch to address the vulnerability. The timeline will depend on the complexity of the issue, but we will provide you with regular updates.
4.  **Public Disclosure:** Once the patch is released, we will coordinate with you to publicly disclose the vulnerability. We are happy to provide credit to researchers for their findings and will issue a CVE if appropriate.

## ❌ Out of Scope

The following issues are generally considered out of scope for our security vulnerability program:

-   Denial of Service (DoS) attacks that require a high volume of traffic.
-   Social engineering or phishing attacks against project maintainers or users.
-   Reports on outdated software versions (please verify against the latest `main` branch).
-   Missing security headers that do not lead to a direct, exploitable vulnerability.

Thank you for helping us keep Authn safe and secure for everyone.
